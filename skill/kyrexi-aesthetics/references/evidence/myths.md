# Myths and contested claims (MYTH)

Popular claims the panel must not use as reasons. Each says what to do instead. Verified against the sources' text on 2026-11-13.

### MYTH-001 · "Red impairs performance" (or makes people anxious and less able)
- grade: Debunked
- applies: refusing red-avoidance or red-for-urgency arguments based on performance
- not-for: —
- value: choose red for meaning, brand or contrast reasons; do not cite performance effects
- source: Gnambs 2020, Limited evidence for the effect of red color on cognitive performance: A meta-analysis · Psychonomic Bulletin & Review · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7704521/ · access: OA · reuse: CC BY 4.0
- verified: 2026-11-13 abstract (full text available)
- caveats: 67 effect sizes from 38 samples; anagram and knowledge tests showed no difference (d = −0.06, −0.04); a reasoning effect (d = −0.34) shrank over time, and after correcting for publication bias no evidential value remained.

### MYTH-002 · "Blue builds trust", "red makes people buy", or any fixed persuasive effect of one hue
- grade: Contested
- applies: refusing single-hue persuasion claims in the decision ledger
- not-for: —
- value: justify colour by audience associations (COL-001), harmony (COL-002), emotional tone (COL-003), contrast (A11Y-001) and brand; never by a promised behaviour change
- source: Elliot 2015, Color and psychological functioning: a review of theoretical and empirical work · Frontiers in Psychology · https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00368 · access: OA · reuse: CC BY 4.0
- source: Jonauskaite & Mohr 2025 (see COL-003) · access: OA · reuse: CC BY 4.0
- verified: 2026-11-13 full text
- caveats: Elliot describes the field as nascent, with underpowered samples and weak control of colour properties, and urges prudence about real-world application; colour–emotion links are many-to-many associations, not behavioural effects.

### MYTH-003 · "Complementary colours are the most harmonious pairing"
- grade: Contested
- applies: refusing colour-wheel harmony as a reason
- not-for: small accents, where a contrasting hue on a background is liked (COL-002)
- value: related hues with lightness contrast for the palette; a contrasting hue only as a small figure
- source: Schloss & Palmer 2011 (see COL-002) · access: OA · reuse: ©
- verified: 2026-11-13 full text
- caveats: pairs with highly contrastive hues were judged neither preferable nor harmonious; no preference gain for complementary pairs after correction.

### MYTH-004 · "Dyslexia fonts (such as OpenDyslexic) help dyslexic readers"
- grade: Contested
- applies: refusing a special dyslexia font as an accessibility measure
- not-for: individual users who choose such a font for themselves
- value: support dyslexic readers with size, spacing (A11Y-005), short lines, plain language and good contrast
- source: Wery & Diliberto 2017, The effect of a specialized dyslexia font, OpenDyslexic, on reading rate and accuracy · Annals of Dyslexia · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5629233/ · access: OA · reuse: ©
- verified: 2026-11-13 abstract
- caveats: single-subject alternating-treatment design with elementary students; no improvement in rate or accuracy versus Arial and Times New Roman, and no student preferred the font. Small study; the null is consistent with other reports in the research notes.

### MYTH-005 · "Hard-to-read fonts improve memory" (desirable difficulty through typography)
- grade: Debunked
- applies: refusing deliberately disfluent type for learning or recall
- not-for: —
- value: make text easy to read
- source: Taylor, Sanson, Burnell, Wade & Garry 2020, Disfluent difficulties are not desirable difficulties: the (lack of) effect of Sans Forgetica on memory · Memory · https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/136656 · access: OA · reuse: ©
- verified: 2026-11-13 abstract
- caveats: four experiments set out to replicate the font creators' unpublished findings; Sans Forgetica did not improve memory over Arial.

### MYTH-006 · "There is one most readable font"
- grade: Contested
- applies: refusing "most readable" as a font rationale
- not-for: —
- value: see TYP-002
- source: Wallace et al. 2022 (see TYP-002) · access: OA · reuse: ©
- verified: 2026-11-13 full text
- caveats: the fastest font differed by reader; preference did not predict speed.

### MYTH-007 · "Animation makes content easier to understand"
- grade: Contested
- applies: refusing motion added for "engagement" or "explanation" without a change to show
- not-for: transitions that show what changed (MOT-002)
- value: see MOT-001
- source: Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt 2002 (see MOT-001) · access: OA · reuse: ©
- verified: 2026-11-13 full text
- caveats: where animation looked better, the comparison usually added information or interactivity.
