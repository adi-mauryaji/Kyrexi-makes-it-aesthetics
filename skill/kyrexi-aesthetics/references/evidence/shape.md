# Evidence · Shape and curvature (SHP)

### SHP-001 · Prefer gently curved forms for objects and symbols seen at a glance; do not expect curvature to rescue a page layout
- grade: A
- applies: buttons, cards, icons, illustrations, logos and other object-like elements, especially for non-expert audiences and first-glance moments
- not-for: page-level spatial composition (the effect was near zero for spatial and architectural designs); expert audiences (small, uncertain effect)
- value: default to modest corner radii and rounded illustration forms; reserve sharp angles for a reason (precision, urgency in a warning, a brand that needs edge)
- source: Chuquichambi, Vartanian, Skov, Corradi, Nadal, Silvia & Munar 2022, meta-analysis of the preference for curvature · Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences 1518(1) · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091794/ · access: OA · reuse: CC BY 4.0
- verified: 2026-11-13 abstract and methods (full text); moderator figures read through a summary of the results section
- caveats: 61 studies, 106 samples, 309 effect sizes, about 11,000 participants; pooled g = 0.39 (medium), no small-study bias. Stronger with brief presentation (g ≈ 0.75 vs 0.32 unlimited) and in non-experts (0.40 vs 0.13 in experts); near zero for spatial designs (interiors, façades). No interface or website studies; corner radius in UIs is an inference.

### SHP-002 · Curved versions of everyday objects are liked more than sharp-angled versions at a glance
- grade: C
- applies: supporting SHP-001 for icon and illustration style
- not-for: —
- value: as SHP-001
- source: Bar & Neta 2006, Humans prefer curved visual objects · Psychological Science 17(8) · https://moshebar.org/_files/ugd/a77db7_5598c791a95a4436960eb2fe671ca1e5.pdf · access: OA (author copy) · reuse: ©
- verified: 2026-11-13 full text
- caveats: 14 participants (18–40), 140 pairs of real objects shown for 84 ms plus meaningless patterns; curved liked more than sharp-angled (effect sizes d = 0.34 to 0.65 across comparisons). Small sample; superseded in weight by the meta-analysis.
